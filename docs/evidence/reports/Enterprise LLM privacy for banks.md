# Banks buy privacy promises, not proofs

The big AI vendors sell banks privacy mainly as **contracts and access policy, not technical guarantees**. Every frontier lab and hyperscaler promises not to train on business data, and most gate zero data retention (ZDR) behind sales approval or special contracts. Pinning processing to a region costs about 10% more. Yet every one of them still processes prompts in plaintext on infrastructure it controls, and in 2026 the most capable models (Anthropic's Covered Models, Google's "Advanced AI" tier, Claude Fable on Bedrock) made 30-day retention mandatory again. Coding-tool vendors now let banks host agent *execution* while keeping inference in their own cloud, and air-gapped vendors keep data at home only by giving up frontier-grade agents. The DLP and AI-security startups, most now owned by Palo Alto Networks, Check Point, SentinelOne, CrowdStrike, F5 and Cisco, detect and substitute sensitive entities, and independent tests put their accuracy far below their marketing claims. Banks respond by running three tiers at once: in-house model brokers over vendor APIs, vendor SaaS under enterprise terms, and self-hosted open-weight models for the most sensitive data. They already send source code to vetted vendor clouds at scale: Citi is rolling Devin out to 40,000 developers, and JPMorgan has made an AI coding tool mandatory for 65,000 technologists. Regulation mostly accepts contractual assurance. The exceptions are card data, Singapore customer information, EU cleartext transfers and duties that require a bank to *demonstrate* what left its perimeter; those reward technical controls. The gap is narrow and real. Nobody sells an agentic coding tool in which a local model handles the sensitive material, a frontier model reasons over abstractions, and leakage is measured. Wells Fargo built that pattern for customer chat and academics built it for question answering, but no one has built or measured it for code. Duet sits exactly in that gap. It still faces vendor roadmaps co-designed with the largest US banks, a cost premium (60–140%) several times the 10–20% banks now pay for locality, and a proof burden it has not yet met: independent audit, detection recall on data nobody planted, certifications, and a support entity.

## Vendors sell contractual privacy while plaintext still leaves the bank

Five kinds of vendors court banks, and each answers one question differently: what happens to a prompt once it leaves the bank? Frontier labs and clouds answer with contract terms. Coding-tool vendors answer with deployment topology, the DLP industry with detectors, and confidential-computing vendors with hardware attestation. Only attestation changes who can read the plaintext, and it does not yet cover the models banks most want.

### Frontier labs promised no training, then reimposed retention for their best models

Every frontier lab and hyperscaler now sells the same baseline: **no training on business data by default, roughly 30 days of retention for abuse monitoring, and zero retention only by special arrangement**. OpenAI "may securely retain API inputs and outputs for up to 30 days". Authorized employees and "specialized third-party contractors" reviewing abuse can access that data ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/enterprise-privacy/)). Both ZDR and Modified Abuse Monitoring require "prior approval by OpenAI", and stateful endpoints such as Assistants, Files, vector stores, Batches and agents sit outside ZDR entirely ([OpenAI data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)). Anthropic keeps commercial Claude Code data for 30 days ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage)) and enables ZDR per organization through sales. It does not block ineligible features such as Files, code execution, the MCP connector or Managed Agents; its docs say "Using one is a choice to step outside your ZDR arrangement." It may also keep flagged inputs and outputs for up to two years even under ZDR ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). The hyperscalers differ in their defaults. Bedrock stores nothing by default under a "zero operator access" model ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/abuse-detection.html)). Azure lets authorized Microsoft employees review flagged samples unless a customer wins Limited Access approval for modified abuse monitoring ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/openai/concepts/abuse-monitoring)). Google exempts Master Agreement customers from abuse logging, but for customers on its online terms it keeps flagged prompts for up to 90 days, outside customer-managed keys ([Google Cloud](https://docs.cloud.google.com/gemini-enterprise-agent-platform/models/abuse-monitoring)).

2026 reversed the trend toward less retention. From **9 June 2026, Anthropic's "Covered Models" (Claude Fable 5/5.1, Mythos 5/5.1) keep prompts and outputs for 30 days even for ZDR customers**. The rule applies on Anthropic's own platform, Bedrock, Google and Foundry alike ([Anthropic Privacy Center](https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/15425996-data-retention-practices-for-covered-models)), and the API rejects a Fable request from any organization that has not enabled retention ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention)). Google's Advanced AI Safety Addendum imposes 30-day storage with possible human review "Regardless of any previous elections or permissions" ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/terms/advanced-ai-safety-addendum)). Bedrock will not serve Fable until the customer switches on its `aws_review` mode: 30 days inside AWS, not shared with Anthropic ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-retention.html)). The most capable models are now the least compatible with "nothing stored", and vendors are competing to fix that.

OpenAI's **Private Safety Processing** has been rolling out since 22 September 2026. It keeps ZDR customers' content "on infrastructure the customer controls" and sends OpenAI only "a narrowly defined signal" when risk is detected. OpenAI added a pointed note that mandatory safety retention, for many organizations, conflicts "with their security obligations" ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/offering-zero-data-retention-for-frontier-models/)). Anthropic's **Enterprise Frontier Safeguards** (EFS), announced in early September 2026, stores activity logs in the customer's own S3, Azure Blob or GCS bucket under customer keys. Anthropic co-developed it with **Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley, Citigroup, Bank of America, Wells Fargo, Mastercard, Visa, Stripe and FIS**. Wells Fargo's CISO summed it up: "We keep custody of our data while Anthropic operates the detection" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/news/enterprise-frontier-safeguards)). Neither mechanism has been independently verified, and The Register's headline on EFS was that customers "must check it worked" ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/09/02/anthropic-promises-zero-data-retention-but-customers-must-check-it-worked/5293789)).

Vendors charge for geography and keys rather than guaranteeing anything. OpenAI charges **a 10% uplift** for data residency on models released after 5 March 2026 ([OpenAI data controls](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data)), and Anthropic charges **1.1x** for US-only inference ([Anthropic](https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/data-residency)). Anthropic's own API offers only "global" or "us" inference, so an EU bank that needs Claude inference inside the EU must go through Bedrock, Google or Azure. Customer-managed keys protect stored history, files and threads, but no vendor document claims they protect a prompt during inference.

On-premises frontier models come from only two vendors. Google offers Gemini on air-gapped Google Distributed Cloud, now also as a Dell-built eight-GPU appliance that holds the model in volatile memory and names financial services as a target market ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/technology/googles-gemini-can-now-run-on-a-single-air-gapped-server-and-vanish-when-you-pull-the-plug)). Microsoft's Foundry Local offers disconnected "large models" to "qualified customers" ([Microsoft](https://blogs.microsoft.com/blog/2026/02/24/microsoft-sovereign-cloud-adds-governance-productivity-and-support-for-large-ai-models-securely-running-even-when-completely-disconnected/)). As of 17 September 2026, AWS's European Sovereign Cloud offered only the open-weight Gemma 4 on Bedrock ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/security/run-open-weight-models-on-aws-bedrock-in-aws-european-sovereign-cloud/)). No on-premises offering from OpenAI or Anthropic was found. Confidential inference is thinner still: Azure's June 2026 catalog lists only the Whisper speech model ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/confidential-computing/overview-azure-products)), and Anthropic's trusted-VM design is "just a sketch of our research" ([Anthropic](https://www.anthropic.com/research/confidential-inference-trusted-vms)).

| Channel | Default retention | Zero-retention access | 2026 rule for top models | Customer-verifiable confidential inference |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| OpenAI API | Up to 30 days (abuse) | Sales approval | Private Safety Processing, phased from Sept 2026 | None found |
| Anthropic API / Claude Code | 30 days | Sales, per organization; flagged data up to 2 years | Covered Models keep 30 days even under ZDR; EFS due fall 2026 | Research sketch |
| Azure OpenAI / Foundry | Abuse monitoring, sampled human review | Limited Access application | Fable available only "Hosted on Anthropic" | Whisper only |
| AWS Bedrock | Nothing stored by default | Default | Fable requires 30-day `aws_review` | Operator-access claim, no customer attestation |
| Google Agent Platform | Flagged prompts up to 90 days (online terms) | Master Agreement or opt-out | 30 days "regardless of any previous elections" | Gemini on air-gapped Distributed Cloud |

Some exposure remains even after a bank buys the strongest tier. In *NYT v. OpenAI*, a court order froze OpenAI API data not covered by ZDR from May to September 2025, while Enterprise and ZDR customers were excluded ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/response-to-nyt-data-demands/)). ZDR is therefore a litigation-exposure control as much as a privacy one. Microsoft France's legal director testified that Microsoft "cannot guarantee" French data against US government requests ([The Register](https://www.theregister.com/off-prem/2025/07/25/microsoft-exec-admits-it-cannot-guarantee-data-sovereignty/458553)). The pattern is consistent across vendors. They **technically enforce policy gating**: which models, regions and features a request may use, as with Bedrock's organization-wide retention-mode controls or Anthropic's `allowed_inference_geos`. But they **govern only by contract what happens to the plaintext** once it arrives. Azure's customer-side check that abuse logging is off is a `ContentLogging: false` flag in a resource description, not a cryptographic proof ([Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/azure/foundry/responsible-ai/openai/data-privacy)).

### Coding-agent vendors moved the hands on-premises and kept the brain in their cloud

By 2026 every major coding-tool vendor sells regulated buyers the same package: no training on business data, SSO and SCIM, audit logs, and SOC 2 or ISO 27001. Every frontier-grade agent (GitHub Copilot, Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Devin, Kiro, Gemini Code Assist) runs inference in a vendor or hyperscaler cloud. The concession to banks is **customer-hosted execution**. Cursor's self-hosted cloud agents (GA 25 March 2026) run tools, code and build artifacts on customer machines, while "inference, planning and orchestration stay in Cursor's cloud" ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/blog/self-hosted-cloud-agents)). Requests pass through Cursor's backend even when customers bring their own API keys ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/data-use)). Devin Outposts (July 2026) run sessions on customer machines while "Devin's agent loop stays in Devin's cloud" ([Devin](https://devin.ai/blog/introducing-devin-outposts)). GitHub added US and EU data-resident inference in April 2026 at a 10% premium-request multiplier. It excluded Gemini models because Google Cloud lacked data-resident endpoints ([GitHub](https://github.blog/changelog/2026-04-13-copilot-data-residency-in-us-eu-and-fedramp-compliance-now-available/)). Claude Code's ZDR is not part of the standard Enterprise plan, and its clients leave plaintext session transcripts under `~/.claude/projects/` for 30 days ([Claude Code docs](https://code.claude.com/docs/en/data-usage)).

The existing controls weaken exactly where banks are scaling agents. GitHub's content exclusion **does not work in VS Code Edit and Agent modes**, and Copilot may still use "semantic information from an excluded file" supplied by the IDE ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/concepts/context/content-exclusion)). Cursor admits that "the terminal and MCP server tools used by Agent cannot block access" to files listed in `.cursorignore`, and that "complete protection isn't guaranteed" ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/docs/context/ignore-files)). **No major coding vendor documents redacting or tokenizing secrets or PII in prompts before inference.** Claude Code redacts only error reports and feedback uploads. Encrypting secrets at the prompt level exists only in third-party proxies such as Stacklok's CodeGate ([DEV/Stacklok](https://dev.to/stacklok/stop-ai-coding-assistants-from-leaking-your-secrets-2mm9)).

Air-gapped coding assistants exist: Tabnine Enterprise, JetBrains AI Enterprise with on-prem Mellum, GitLab Duo Self-Hosted, Mistral Code, Qodo, and Sourcegraph with a self-hosted model. Each one gives up the agentic tier. Windsurf put self-hosting into maintenance mode in May 2025 because its agentic features "simply cannot be run entirely in a self-hosted environment" and frontier models would need "a full rack of frontier GPUs" ([Devin](https://devin.ai/blog/self-hosted-deployment-maintenance-mode)). GitLab does not support models with "limited functionality", and it sizes a 22B model at two A100 80GB GPUs ([GitLab](https://docs.gitlab.com/administration/gitlab_duo_self_hosted/supported_models_and_hardware_requirements/)).

The capability gap is measurable. On Terminal-Bench 2.0, GPT-5.5 scores **82.7%** and Claude Sonnet 5 80.4%. The best open-weight model (GLM-5.1) scores **69.0%**, and **Qwen3.6-27B scores 59.3%**. These numbers come from an aggregate that mixes vendor-reported and third-party runs ([llm-stats](https://llm-stats.com/benchmarks/terminal-bench-2)). Epoch puts open weights about four months behind the closed frontier overall ([Epoch AI](https://epoch.ai/data-insights/open-closed-eci-gap)). Stanford's Hazy Research concludes that "browser-based agentic tasks and coding still favor cloud" ([Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2026-05-15-minions-to-openjarvis-retrospective)). The only bank publicly running a hybrid coding setup is Spain's **Abanca**. It prototypes in the cloud on Mistral Code "while core banking code stays on-prem" ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-code/)).

List prices sit at $19–60 per seat per month — Copilot Business $19 and Enterprise $39 ([GitHub Docs](https://docs.github.com/en/copilot/get-started/plans)), Claude Enterprise a seat fee plus usage "at standard API rates" ([Claude](https://claude.com/pricing)) — while self-hosted options are sold through sales, with the buyer paying for the GPUs. Vendor churn is a risk factor in its own right. Windsurf became "Devin Desktop" through an over-the-air update in June 2026 ([Devin](https://devin.ai/blog/windsurf-is-now-devin-desktop)), Amazon Q Developer's IDE plugins reach end of support in April 2027 ([AWS](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/devops/amazon-q-developer-end-of-support-announcement/)), and Tricentis bought Tabnine in July 2026, whose pricing pages now redirect to Tricentis ([BusinessWire](https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20260730388427/en/Tricentis-Acquires-Tabnine-to-Further-Scale-Agentic-Quality-Engineering-for-the-Enterprise)). A bank that picked a tool in 2023–24 because it could be self-hosted has probably seen that choice disrupted.

### Privacy middleware detects and substitutes, and leaks badly on code

The DLP and AI-security market sells four kinds of product — workforce "shadow AI" DLP inside SASE proxies and browsers, runtime guardrails in front of in-house AI apps, AI gateways with PII plugins, and privacy vaults that tokenize data and restore it later — and most of the standalone firms have been bought:

| Buyer | Target | Price |
|---|---|---|
| Palo Alto Networks | Protect AI | reported **$634.5M** ([BankInfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/blogs/ai-security-goes-mainstream-as-vendors-spend-heavily-on-ma-p-3953)) |
| Cisco | Robust Intelligence | about $400M ([BankInfoSecurity](https://www.bankinfosecurity.com/blogs/ai-security-goes-mainstream-as-vendors-spend-heavily-on-ma-p-3953)) |
| SentinelOne | Prompt Security | $159.3M fair value ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1583708/000158370825000159/s-20251031.htm)) |
| F5 | CalypsoAI | $180M ([SEC](https://www.sec.gov/Archives/edgar/data/1048695/000104869525000157/ffiv-20250930.htm)) |
| Check Point | Lakera | not stated ([Check Point](https://www.checkpoint.com/press-releases/check-point-acquires-lakera-to-deliver-end-to-end-ai-security-for-enterprises/)) |
| CrowdStrike | Pangea | not stated ([SecurityWeek](https://www.securityweek.com/crowdstrike-to-acquire-pangea-to-launch-ai-detection-and-response-aidr/)) |
| Cato | Aim Security | not stated ([Calcalist](https://www.calcalistech.com/ctechnews/article/7pzhe3mrd)) |

The buyers are network and endpoint platforms, which means the market treats AI-traffic inspection as a SASE feature rather than a standalone product. The survivors (Harmonic, Nightfall, Protecto, Skyflow, Limina, Lasso) focus on data protection. Named bank customers are rare: Citi uses Lakera and invested in it ([Fortune](https://fortune.com/2024/07/24/lakera-20-million-funding-ai-chatbot-security)), and Bank of Muscat uses Protecto ([Protecto](https://www.protecto.ai/)). Demand is plain from Harmonic's telemetry: **4.37% of prompts and about 22% of files** sent to GenAI tools contained sensitive content ([Morningstar/BusinessWire](https://www.morningstar.com/news/business-wire/20250731456105/22-of-all-files-and-437-of-prompts-submitted-to-genai-tools-by-employees-contain-sensitive-data)).

Nearly every product runs the same pipeline. It detects entities, then blocks them, masks them one way, or substitutes a token that is swapped back into the response. Cloud-native guardrails mask one way only; Bedrock, for example, replaces a name with `{NAME}`. Bedrock's own documentation admits three gaps: it does not check tool-call arguments or results, it keeps the original unmasked input in invocation logs, and it returns raw PII in trace fields ([AWS](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-sensitive-filters.html)). Local and small models do appear, but **only as detectors**. OpenAI's Apache-licensed Privacy Filter (1.5B parameters, 50M active) runs on the user's machine and includes a class for secrets ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/openai/privacy-filter)). Cloudflare runs Presidio and Llama3-70B on its own network so prompts "are not sent to third-party vendors" ([Cloudflare](https://blog.cloudflare.com/ai-prompt-protection/)). No commercial vendor was found whose local model *performs* the sensitive part of the task.

Independent tests fall far short of vendors' 95–99.9% accuracy claims:

| Evidence | Result | Source |
|---|---|---|
| Open benchmark, four datasets including financial text | Best open detectors average **F1 0.48–0.54**; Piiranha drops 78% outside its training domain; names in financial text at F1 0.139 | [Sikkema](https://albertsikkema.com/python/security/privacy/2026/06/01/benchmarking-open-source-pii-detection.html) |
| Context-dependent PII in multi-turn chats | **Presidio F1 0.086**; recall falls from 0.975 under 1,000 characters to 0.687 over 10,000 | [PII-TRACE](https://arxiv.org/html/2609.22200) |
| Local processing + redaction + rephrasing | 0.6% PII leak, but **31.3% leak on proprietary code** | [LLM-Redactor](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.12064) |
| Anonymization cost to utility | GPT-4o mini retrieval score falls from 0.80 to 0.32; reversible pseudonyms beat redaction | [arXiv 2609.11335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.11335) |
| Inference without identifiers | GPT-4 infers personal attributes at 85.5% top-1 accuracy | [arXiv 2310.07298](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.07298) |
| Guardrail evasion | Character injection fully bypasses several commercial guardrails | [arXiv 2504.11168](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.11168) |
| Secret scanners | Gitleaks reaches 88% recall at 46% precision | [arXiv 2307.00714](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.00714) |

For source code the conclusion is blunt. The code *is* the intellectual property, so these products block it, coach the user, or redact only the secrets inside it.

### Confidential computing covers open weights, and hybrid delegation stays in papers

GPU trusted execution environments (TEEs) are production-ready. A tuned B200 plus Intel TDX stack loses about **1–3% of throughput**, though an untuned stack loses 30–40% ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.26575)). NVIDIA reports 96.1–98.2% of baseline throughput for DeepSeek-R1 on eight B200s ([NVIDIA](https://developer.nvidia.com/blog/enabling-private-high-performance-production-ai-inference-with-nvidia-confidential-computing/)). The commercial services built on this serve **open-weight models one tier below the frontier**. Edgeless Systems' Privatemode counts Capgemini's confidential coding assistants among its users and claims BSI C5 ([Edgeless](https://www.edgeless.systems/press-release-edgeless-systems-nvidia-confidential-ai); [Privatemode](https://www.privatemode.ai/)). Tinfoil open-sources its security-critical stack ([Tinfoil](https://tinfoil.sh/technology)). Proprietary frontier models run inside attested enclaves in only two places. One is Google's air-gapped Gemini appliance. The other is Apple's Private Cloud Compute, which runs Gemini-derived Siri models with Google excluded ([9to5Mac](https://9to5mac.com/2026/01/29/apple-confirms-gemini-powered-siri-will-use-private-cloud-compute/)) and now also runs on Google Cloud TDX and Blackwell hardware ([Google Cloud](https://cloud.google.com/blog/products/identity-security/powering-the-next-era-of-confidential-ai)).

TEEs remove the cloud operator from the parties a bank must trust. They do not remove the chip vendors or the attested code itself. The TEE.fail interposer, built from under $1,000 of hardware, extracted attestation keys from fully updated TDX and SEV-SNP machines ([TEE.fail](https://tee.fail/)). DDRop, built from about $159 of parts, took full control of Intel TDX in September 2026, and Intel and AMD called physical attacks out of scope ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2026/09/new-ddrop-attack-breaks-intel-tdx-and.html)). Trail of Bits found **28 issues, 8 of them high-severity**, in Meta's production private-inference system before launch ([Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/04/what-we-learned-about-tee-security-from-auditing-whatsapps-private-inference/)).

The architecture closest to duet lives only in research. Stanford's MinionS keeps **97.9% of GPT-4o's accuracy at 5.7x lower cloud cost** by having a local model read long documents and send extracts to the cloud model. Its authors state "we do not address privacy concerns", and derived content still leaves the device ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15964v1)). PAPILLON uses a local model to rewrite queries for a cloud model and recombine the answers. With Llama-3.1-8B it reaches 85.5% quality with **7.5% leakage**; with a 1B model, leakage is 39.3% ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2410.17127)). Follow-up work in 2026 shows that stripping explicit identifiers misses quasi-identifying self-disclosures ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.09140)), and that agents with web search can re-identify anonymized people ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.30848)). Split inference is worse: the input can be reconstructed from its activations despite noise defenses ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.23158)). Local models also fail in characteristic ways; in the MinionS study their accuracy dropped 56 points when instructions had several sequential steps ([arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2502.15964v1)). Critically, **no paper found measures how much local sanitization leaks on code or agentic software-engineering tasks**. Every benchmark is question answering or chat.

## Banks run three tiers at once and ship source code to vetted clouds

The ban era was short and procedural. In February 2023 JPMorgan, Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, Bank of America, Wells Fargo and Deutsche Bank blocked or limited ChatGPT through their existing third-party-software controls. JPMorgan's move "was not triggered by any incident" ([Retail Banker International](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/news/banking-majors-bar-chatgpt-use/); [Bloomberg](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-02-24/citigroup-goldman-sachs-join-chatgpt-crackdown-fn-reports)). Three years later the same banks are among the heaviest enterprise users. **More than 200,000 JPMorgan employees use its LLM Suite**, with over 100 GenAI solutions in production ([JPMorganChase](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/ir/annual-report/2025/ar-ceo-letters)). Wells Fargo is rolling Google's Agentspace out to about 215,000 staff ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/wells-fargo-plans-to-unleash-googles-agentic-ai-bank-wide)), and more than 80% of Deutsche Bank's workforce uses GenAI tools ([Deutsche Bank](https://www.db.com/what-we-do/focus-topics/tech/index?language_id=1)).

Most large banks now run three deployment archetypes side by side, which implies a routing decision for each data class. The first is an **in-house model broker** that owns the interface, retrieval, logging and routing while calling vendor models under enterprise terms: JPMorgan's LLM Suite over OpenAI and Anthropic models ([AI Magazine](https://aimagazine.com/news/jpmorgan-openai-anthropic-evolving-banking-operations)), Goldman's GS AI Platform over GPT, Gemini and Llama ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/01/21/goldman-sachs-launches-ai-assistant.html)), Citi's Stylus, UBS's Red and Deutsche Bank's dbLumina. The second is **vendor SaaS at scale, governed by contract**: ChatGPT Enterprise for all 120,000 BBVA employees ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/bbva-collaboration-expansion/)) and about 50,000 at Commonwealth Bank of Australia ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/commonwealth-bank-of-australia/)), Microsoft 365 Copilot for 100,000 staff at Barclays ([Microsoft](https://ukstories.microsoft.com/features/barclays-rolls-out-microsoft-365-copilot-to-100000-colleagues/)) and about 50,000 licences at UBS ([UBS](https://www.ubs.com/global/en/our-firm/what-we-do/technology/innovation-and-ai.html)), and a Morgan Stanley advisor assistant that rests on OpenAI's zero-retention policy ([OpenAI](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/)). The third is **self-hosted open or sovereign-provider weights** where sensitivity or sovereignty dominates. BNP Paribas's LLM-as-a-Service runs in group data centres ([FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/bnp-paribas-provides-its-businesses-with-an-llm-as-a-service-platform-to-accelerate-the-industrialization-of-generative-ai-use-cases)) with Mistral on-premises for processes such as KYC ([BNP Paribas](https://group.bnpparibas/en/press-release/bnp-paribas-and-mistral-ai-extend-their-partnership-to-support-the-next-phase-of-generative-ai-deployment-within-the-group)); HSBC self-hosts Mistral models "on HSBC's internal technology systems", "ensuring full ownership of data" ([HSBC](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2025/hsbc-and-mistral-ai-join-forces-to-accelerate-ai-adoption-across-global-bank)); and Capital One builds on fine-tuned Llama because "we view our data as a huge advantage" ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/why-capital-one-built-its-multi-agent-ai-platform-around-open-weight-models)).

BNP's new five-year Google deal (24 September 2026) states the policy most plainly. In 2025 the bank "had not placed client data or production environments containing sensitive data in public cloud", and under the new deal "**certain data categories still excluded**" ([The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/bnp-paribas-adds-google-cloud-ai-while-retaining-limits-on-public-cloud-data)).

At most banks, source code is not one of those excluded categories. Citi gave AI coding tools to 30,000 developers by early 2025 ([CIO Dive](https://www.ciodive.com/news/citi-bank-modernization-generative-ai-coding-assistant/737624/)) and began rolling Cognition's Devin out to its 40,000 developers in July 2025 ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/citi-is-rolling-out-agentic-ai-to-its-40-000-developers)). JPMorgan told about **65,000 technologists in March 2026 that using an approved AI coding tool was no longer optional**, capped Claude Code spend at $2,000 a month for some engineers, and is moving the tool into a "more secure" environment called Devspace ([Newsquawk, summarizing Business Insider](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/jpmorgan-jpm-introduced-usd-2k-monthly-claude-code-spending-limits-for-some-engineers-and-is-rolling-out-a-more-secure-devspace-environment-business-insider-reports)). Goldman is piloting Devin alongside 12,000 developers ([CNBC](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/07/11/goldman-sachs-autonomous-coder-pilot-marks-major-ai-milestone.html)); Morgan Stanley built DevGen.AI on OpenAI models and used it to review 9 million lines of legacy code ([Slashdot/WSJ](https://developers.slashdot.org/story/25/06/04/1233253/morgan-stanley-says-its-ai-tool-processed-9-million-lines-of-legacy-code-this-year-and-saved-280000-developer-hours)); HSBC has about 31,000 engineers on a GenAI coding assistant ([WatersTechnology](https://www.waterstechnology.com/emerging-technologies/7952964/hsbc-gives-31000-engineers-an-ai-coding-assistant)); more than 15,000 Deutsche Bank developers use Gemini Code Assist and GitHub Copilot ([Deutsche Bank](https://www.db.com/what-we-do/focus-topics/tech/index?language_id=1)); and Wells Fargo uses Claude for coding ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/wells-fargos-ai-assistant-just-crossed-245-million-interactions-with-zero-humans-in-the-loop-and-zero-pii-to-the-llm)).

No bank found says publicly that its code goes only to on-premises models. Banks contain **what agents can do**, not **what models can see**. Citi's CTO says "we're certainly not allowing any kind of deployment of code by the agents", and its agents are grounded only on internal repositories so they "cannot swim out into the web" ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/citi-is-rolling-out-agentic-ai-to-its-40-000-developers)). The payoff is modest and self-reported: Evident finds average productivity gains of 10–20% ([Evident](https://evidentinsights.com/bankingbrief/heres-the-2025-evident-ai-index/)).

The one named hybrid system in production is not a coding tool. Wells Fargo's Fargo assistant transcribes speech locally, detects and tokenizes PII with an internal small language model, sends only de-identified text to Gemini Flash, and converts tokens back to real values in-house. It handled **245 million interactions in 2024**, and CIO Chintan Mehta says "No sensitive data ever reaches the model" and "We're the filters in front and behind" ([VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/wells-fargos-ai-assistant-just-crossed-245-million-interactions-with-zero-humans-in-the-loop-and-zero-pii-to-the-llm)). No bank documents the same pattern for code.

Bank risk leaders frame the problem around integration and dependence more than model training. JPMorgan CISO Patrick Opet's April 2025 open letter to suppliers warned that the growth of AI and agent services "amplifies and rapidly distributes these risks". He attacked "single-factor explicit trust between systems on the internet and private internal resources", demanded "secure by default configurations", and urged customers to "reject these integration models without better solutions" ([JPMorganChase](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/about/technology/blog/open-letter-to-our-suppliers)).

The surveys point the same way. The Bank of England and FCA found data protection and privacy to be **the largest perceived regulatory constraint on AI**, with four of the top five perceived risks related to data, the top three providers supplying 44% of the models in use, and 46% of firms reporting only "partial understanding" of the AI they use ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2024/artificial-intelligence-in-uk-financial-services-2024)). EY puts data privacy at 22% of banks' stated barriers to GenAI ([EY](https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/banking-capital-markets/ai-in-banking-ey-parthenon-genai-survey-insights)). A vendor-reported endpoint-DLP deployment at an unnamed regional investment bank blocked client portfolio information, trading strategies, material non-public information and deal data ([CFOtech](https://cfotech.news/story/bank-blocks-confidential-data-leaks-to-public-ai-tools)) — semantic categories that entity detectors barely touch.

The live incidents come from outside sanctioned tools. In May 2026 an employee at Community Bank, a regional lender in Pennsylvania, Ohio and West Virginia, fed customer names, birth dates and Social Security numbers into an unauthorized AI app ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/12/us-bank-discloses-security-lapse-after-sharing-customer-data-with-ai-app/)). That single act triggered an SEC 8-K filing, a 36-hour notice to the regulator, and GLBA notices to customers ([American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/a-bank-breaks-its-silence-on-its-shadow-ai-breach)). No leak through a sanctioned big-bank AI platform was found.

## Regulation demands proof only for specific data classes

No US, EU, UK or Singapore regulator has an AI-specific rule on sending data to a model provider. No financial regulator was found to have penalized a firm specifically for leaking data through GenAI prompts. The obligations come from general security, privacy and third-party rules. A bank can satisfy most of them with contracts, but not all.

US law leans on contracts, and model governance has just loosened. The GLBA safeguards guidelines require contracts obliging service providers to implement safeguards ([eCFR](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-12/chapter-III/subchapter-B/part-364/appendix-Appendix%20B%20to%20Part%20364)), and the 2023 interagency third-party guidance says outsourcing "does not diminish" a bank's responsibility ([Federal Reserve](https://www.federalreserve.gov/newsevents/pressreleases/bcreg20230606a.htm)). On **17 April 2026 the OCC, Fed and FDIC rescinded SR 11-7** and issued principles-based guidance stating "Generative AI and agentic AI models... are not within the scope of this guidance" ([OCC](https://www.occ.gov/news-issuances/bulletins/2026/bulletin-2026-13.html)), so coding agents now fall to information-security and vendor-risk examiners. New York's Department of Financial Services tells firms to require AI vendors to "take advantage of available enhanced privacy, security, and confidentiality options" — in practice a ZDR tier — while also monitoring for "unusual query behaviors that might indicate an attempt to extract NPI" and listing AI systems in asset inventories from November 2025 ([NYDFS](https://www.dfs.ny.gov/industry-guidance/industry-letters/il20241016-cyber-risks-ai-and-strategies-combat-related-risks)). Amended Regulation S-P requires service providers to report breaches within 72 hours ([Holland & Knight](https://www.hklaw.com/en/insights/publications/2026/05/regulation-s-p-amendments-compliance-deadline-approaching)), and FINRA's 2026 report warns that agents "may unintentionally store, explore, disclose, or misuse sensitive or proprietary information" and recommends tracking agent actions ([FINRA](https://www.finra.org/sites/default/files/2025-12/2026-annual-regulatory-oversight-report.pdf)). Federal policy is otherwise deregulatory: a December 2025 executive order targets state AI laws, and Colorado repealed and narrowed its AI Act in May 2026 ([Skadden](https://www.skadden.com/insights/publications/2026/06/colorado-repeals-and-replaces-its-ai-act)).

The EU stacks more binding layers. Since 17 January 2025, **DORA** treats any LLM API supporting a critical or important function as an ICT third-party service that needs a register entry, contract clauses on data location and confidentiality, exit strategies and concentration analysis ([EUR-Lex](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2022/2554/oj)). The 19 critical providers designated for direct EU oversight in November 2025 include AWS, Google Cloud and Microsoft ([EBA](https://www.eba.europa.eu/publications-and-media/press-releases/european-supervisory-authorities-designate-critical-ict-third-party-providers-under-digital)) but, per a secondary list, no pure-play model lab ([regulation-dora.eu](https://www.regulation-dora.eu/blog/critical-ict-third-party-designations-october-2025)), so a bank calling OpenAI or Anthropic directly carries the whole due-diligence burden itself. The ECB's July 2025 cloud guide asks for encryption "in transit, at rest and, where feasible, in use" and for costed exit plans before go-live ([Jones Day](https://www.jonesday.com/en/insights/2025/11/new-ecb-guide-on-outsourcing-cloud-services-to-cloud-service-providers)). Under GDPR, the European Data Protection Board found **no effective technical supplementary measure** when a third-country importer needs data in the clear and faces overbroad public-authority access, while pseudonymised data with the key kept in the EEA can work ([EDPB](https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/recommendations/recommendations-012020-measures-supplement-transfer_en)); the EU–US Data Privacy Framework currently removes that problem for certified US vendors, but the framework is under legal challenge. The EU Court of Justice's *EDPS v SRB* judgment of 4 September 2025 held that pseudonymised data may not be personal data **for a recipient that cannot re-identify it**, though it remains personal data for the controller ([CURIA](https://curia.europa.eu/site/upload/docs/application/pdf/2025-09/cp250107en.pdf)); the Commission's attempt to write this into GDPR stalled after a Council compromise dropped it ([IAPP](https://iapp.org/news/a/eu-member-states-leaked-digital-omnibus-compromise-proposal-eliminates-revised-gdpr-definition-of-personal-data)). The AI Act adds little for coding tools: general-purpose model duties have fallen on providers since August 2025, and high-risk obligations for credit scoring were pushed to 2 December 2027 ([Gibson Dunn](https://www.gibsondunn.com/eu-ai-act-omnibus-agreement-postponed-high-risk-deadlines-and-other-key-changes/)).

**United Kingdom.** The UK regulates AI through technology-neutral rules. The FCA said in January 2026 that it "does not currently plan to introduce AI-specific rules" ([Covington](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/04/uk-financial-services-regulators-approach-to-artificial-intelligence-in-2026/)), and PRA SS2/21 governs outsourcing ([PRA](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/prudential-regulation/publication/2021/march/outsourcing-and-third-party-risk-management-ss)). HM Treasury's first critical-third-party designations, on 10 July 2026, named AWS, Google Cloud, Microsoft and Oracle but no model labs ([Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/news/2026/july/uk-financial-regulators-to-begin-overseeing-critical-third-parties-announced-by-hmt)), despite a parliamentary committee's call to add AI providers ([Covington](https://www.globalpolicywatch.com/2026/04/uk-financial-services-regulators-approach-to-artificial-intelligence-in-2026/)).

**Singapore.** Section 47 of the Banking Act is a statutory gate: customer information may be disclosed only under conditions the Act lists ([Singapore Statutes](https://sso.agc.gov.sg/Act/BA1970)). MAS's proposed AI risk-management guidelines, not final as of September 2026, explicitly cover "Generative AI and AI agents" and set expectations for AI inventories and third-party risk ([MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/news/media-releases/2025/mas-guidelines-for-artificial-intelligence-risk-management)).

**Card payments.** The PCI Security Standards Council's September 2025 AI Principles say AI "should not be trusted with high-impact secrets or unprotected sensitive data". They recommend payment tokens or single-use card numbers (PANs) and ask firms to log "prompt inputs and reasoning process" ([PCI SSC](https://blog.pcisecuritystandards.org/ai-principles-securing-the-use-of-ai-in-payment-environments)).

| Driver | Jurisdiction | What it asks for | Can a vendor contract satisfy it? |
|---|---|---|---|
| Interagency TPRM (2023), GLBA safeguards | US | Due diligence, contracted safeguards, monitoring | Largely yes |
| NYDFS Part 500 and AI letter (Oct 2024) | New York | Enhanced vendor privacy options; monitoring for customer-data extraction; AI in asset inventory | Vendor terms yes; monitoring is technical |
| DORA Arts. 9, 28, 30; ECB cloud guide | EU | Register, clauses, exit plans, concentration analysis, leakage prevention, encryption in use "where feasible" | Clauses yes; exit and leakage prevention need technology |
| GDPR transfers (EDPB 01/2020) and *SRB* | EU | Cleartext abroad has no technical fix; local-key pseudonymisation can work | No |
| PCI DSS 4.0.1 and AI Principles (2025) | Card data | Tokens, sanitisation, prompt logging | No |
| Banking Act s.47 | Singapore | Customer information disclosed only under statutory conditions | No |
| OCC 2026-13 / SR 26-2 | US | Model-risk guidance that excludes GenAI | Not applicable today |

In practice, **contractual ZDR plus an enterprise data-processing agreement is enough for ordinary source code in the US and UK**, which is why banks send code to vetted clouds. Technical guarantees become decisive for four data classes: card data and secrets, Singapore customer information, EU personal data that would travel in cleartext to a processor outside the Data Privacy Framework, and anything a bank must *demonstrate* rather than *assert* left its perimeter under logging and monitoring duties. Two legal questions remain open: no regulator has said whether vendor ZDR terms are enough, and no supervisor has tested the *SRB* relative-pseudonymisation reasoning against LLM API traffic.

## Duet fits the excluded-data gap but owes banks evidence it lacks

### The empty quadrant: frontier agents without plaintext egress

Laid side by side, the market forces a choice between frontier-grade agents and keeping sensitive plaintext at home.

| Approach | Frontier-grade agent | What the model provider sees | Examples |
|---|---|---|---|
| Frontier agent under enterprise terms | Yes | Plaintext, protected by contract | Copilot, Claude Code, Codex, Cursor, Devin |
| Customer-hosted execution | Yes | Plaintext prompts; only tools run at home | Cursor self-hosted agents, Devin Outposts |
| DLP gateway in front of a frontier agent | Yes | Plaintext minus what detectors catch | Prompt Security, Nightfall, Kong, LiteLLM |
| Air-gapped assistant on open weights | No | Nothing | Tabnine, JetBrains with Mellum, GitLab Duo Self-Hosted, Mistral Code |
| Open weights in a TEE | One tier below | Encrypted data outside an attested enclave | Privatemode, Tinfoil |
| Proprietary model on an on-prem appliance | Gemini only; not an agent product | Nothing | Gemini on Google Distributed Cloud |
| Duet (as reported by the project) | Non-inferior to frontier alone on its own benchmark | Placeholders, handles, local-model summaries | — |

In duet's design, a local model such as Qwen 27B reads sensitive material on the customer's hardware, the frontier model plans and decides from placeholders, handles and summaries, commands run in an OS sandbox that hides sensitive paths, and every outbound byte lands in a hash-chained audit log. Among the coding agents reviewed, it is the only design that tries to fill the empty row. Its central bet also sidesteps what killed Windsurf's self-hosted product: the local model does not have to be a frontier-grade agent, because it does not decide. A 27B-class model that scores 59.3% on Terminal-Bench 2.0 is a poor autonomous coder but can be a competent reader and summarizer, provided its tasks stay short and single-step — the regime where MinionS found local models reliable.

Several of the incumbents' documented weaknesses map directly onto duet's mechanisms. The sandbox that hides sensitive paths from every command answers the hole GitHub and Cursor admit — exclusion lists that agent modes, terminals and MCP tools route around — with an operating-system control instead of an advisory file. Placeholder-only egress lets a bank use a retention-mandatory model such as Claude Fable without putting plaintext secrets or customer data in the 30-day safety store, a vendor-neutral answer to the problem EFS and Private Safety Processing solve one vendor at a time. A customer-held, tamper-evident record of every outbound byte is the kind of evidence NYDFS monitoring, PCI prompt logging, FINRA's "track agent actions" and a DORA register of which vendor received which data classes all call for, and it is stronger than a `ContentLogging: false` flag or a vendor's promise that a customer can "check it worked". If PANs, secrets and customer identifiers never reach the frontier vendor, that vendor stays out of PCI scope, Singapore's section 47 is never engaged, and the flow resembles the local-key pseudonymisation that the EDPB and *SRB* favour. Duet does not remove DORA obligations for the frontier API itself — the register entry and contract clauses remain — but it shrinks what that vendor receives, and if duet can fall back to local-only operation, a bank's exit plan has something concrete to point at.

The natural buyers already have a policy that says "certain data categories still excluded": European banks with sovereignty mandates such as BNP Paribas, HSBC and Abanca; Singapore banks under banking secrecy; teams whose repositories, test fixtures and logs contain card or customer data; and mid-size banks without a JPMorgan-scale in-house broker. The evidence points to systems integrators as the channel: Capgemini runs Mistral Code on-premises for more than 1,500 developers serving regulated clients ([Mistral](https://mistral.ai/news/mistral-code/)) and uses Privatemode for confidential coding assistants ([Edgeless](https://www.edgeless.systems/press-release-edgeless-systems-nvidia-confidential-ai)). And because no published study measures how much local sanitization leaks on coding tasks, duet's canary-and-proxy measurement setup could produce the first public evidence in the category — a research contribution and a sales asset at once.

### Incumbents win on scale, paperwork, price and co-design

Candidly, most of the market does not need what duet sells. Banks already send ordinary source code to vetted clouds, and the incumbents are closing the remaining gaps with the largest banks as co-designers: Anthropic built EFS with Goldman, Morgan Stanley, Citi, Bank of America and Wells Fargo; OpenAI is moving ZDR content onto customer-controlled infrastructure; GitHub, Cursor and Devin keep adding data residency and customer-hosted execution; and Google will put Gemini in an air-gapped box. Tier-1 banks also build their own privacy layers — Wells Fargo's "filters in front and behind" is a duet-like pattern built in-house — so for JPMorgan-scale buyers duet is more plausibly a reference design or a component than a product.

The paperwork gap is large. SOC 2 Type II and ISO 27001 are table stakes, and ISO 42001 is becoming the standard evidence of AI governance: Cursor lists SOC 2 Type II, ISO 27001, ISO 42001 and AIUC-1 ([Cursor](https://cursor.com/security)), and Augment claims the first ISO 42001 certification for a coding assistant ([Augment](https://www.augmentcode.com/blog/augment-code-is-the-first-ai-coding-assistant-to-be-iso-iec-42001-certified)). Copilot, Kiro and Gemini Code Assist offer IP indemnity. The acquisition wave shows that banks buy AI security from platforms they already own, and churn among coding vendors teaches third-party-risk teams to weigh vendor viability heavily. Duet's GPL-3.0 licence cuts both ways: banks can read every line, the same transparency Apple and Tinfoil rely on ([Apple](https://security.apple.com/blog/private-cloud-compute/)), but copyleft complicates any vendor or integrator that wants to embed and resell it, and a bank's open-source office will want a named company behind support, patches and vulnerability disclosure.

Price is the sharpest problem. Banks today pay **10–20% for locality** — OpenAI's 10% residency uplift, Anthropic's 1.1x for US-only inference, Copilot's 1.1x data-resident multiplier, and about 20% more for Kiro on GovCloud ([Kiro](https://kiro.dev/faq/)). Duet's measured premium of **1.6–2.4x the frontier alone** is three to fourteen times that, before counting the GPUs that run the local model, and on-premises coding-AI costs are dominated by GPU hardware and operations, as GitLab's sizing shows ([GitLab](https://docs.gitlab.com/administration/gitlab_duo_self_hosted/supported_models_and_hardware_requirements/)). Under JPMorgan's $2,000 monthly Claude Code cap, a 1.6–2.4x multiplier would buy roughly 42–63% of the work the same budget buys today.

Duet's quality result is encouraging but comes from its own benchmark, and banks already discount self-reported numbers. Finally, the incident that actually hit a bank in 2026 came from shadow AI. Duet does nothing to stop that; it remains the ground of SASE and DLP vendors.

### Seven proofs a bank risk committee will demand

| What the bank will ask | Duet's evidence today | What would satisfy a risk committee |
|---|---|---|
| Does sensitive data leave? | Zero leaks flagged by planted canaries and an independent leak proxy across its own benchmark runs | Third-party leakage tests on bank-like code, fixtures and logs, including unplanted and quasi-identifying data |
| How much does detection miss? | Known coverage gaps | Published recall and precision per data class (PANs, secrets, PII, customer IDs) on public corpora |
| Is the local model doing the reading? | Underused: it reads only sensitive files, about 8% of tokens in duet's Gate 2 runs | Runs showing the local model handles most reading of sensitive inputs, with leakage measured on its summaries |
| Can we prove what left? | Hash-chained log of every outbound byte | Independent check that no egress path bypasses the recorder; SIEM export; retention controls |
| Is quality good enough? | Non-inferior to frontier alone on its own benchmark | Replication on public agentic benchmarks and a bank pilot |
| What does it cost? | 1.6–2.4x frontier alone | A premium near the 10–20% banks pay for locality, or a business case for work that is otherwise not permitted |
| Who stands behind it? | GPL-3.0 open-source project | Certifications for any service component, signed builds and an SBOM, a support contract, DORA-ready terms |

Four of these are harder than they look.

**Canaries prove the detector catches what was planted; banks will ask about what nobody planted.** Independent benchmarks put the best open detectors near F1 0.5, and they collapse out of domain. PII-TRACE shows the same span can be sensitive in one context and harmless in another. The categories banks block most (trading strategies, material non-public information, deal terms) are semantic and beyond any entity detector. Duet's recently found coverage gaps belong to that same failure class.

The summary channel is the subtler risk. The MinionS authors warn that derived content leaves the device, PAPILLON's best configuration still leaked 7.5% of private units, quasi-identifiers survive when explicit identifiers are stripped, and redaction-based pipelines leaked 31.3% of proprietary code. Duet therefore needs a semantic leakage metric covering identifiers, business logic and code structure in summaries, not just exact-string matches.

**An underused local model means the differentiating mechanism is only lightly exercised.** In duet's Gate 2 runs the local model handled about 8% of tokens: summaries and answers about sensitive files, while the frontier did all other reading. The zero-leak result therefore says most about the placeholder, detector and sandbox layers, and only a little about the split between a local model that reads and a frontier model that decides. Until the local model carries more of the reading, neither the privacy result nor the cost premium can be fully attributed to the architecture a bank would buy.

**A hash chain proves the log was not edited, not that it is complete.** The Trail of Bits audit of Meta's private inference found its high-severity flaws in what the attestation failed to measure: environment variables loaded after measurement, unmeasured ACPI tables, and replayable reports ([Security Boulevard](https://securityboulevard.com/2026/04/what-we-learned-about-tee-security-from-auditing-whatsapps-private-inference/)). Hazy Research's own Secure Minions carries the caveat that it "has not undergone a third-party audit" ([Hazy Research](https://hazyresearch.stanford.edu/blog/2025-05-12-security)). Duet needs the equivalent: an outside review showing that no child process, DNS lookup, telemetry path or tool call reaches the network around the recorder. The review should also cover what stays on the endpoint. The placeholder-to-value map, local transcripts and local-model context are now the crown jewels. Claude Code's plaintext transcripts are already a cited weakness, and under PCI any local store holding PANs must render them unreadable.

**The legal theory is untested.** The *SRB* reasoning has never been applied to LLM traffic. Outside counsel opinions and a supervised pilot would turn a plausible argument into one a bank can cite. The FCA's AI Live Testing and Singapore's Project MindForge ([MAS](https://www.mas.gov.sg/schemes-and-initiatives/project-mindforge)) are natural venues.

## Conclusion

Vendors are converging on customer-held evidence: logs in the customer's bucket, content on customer-controlled infrastructure. They are converging because 2026 showed that the most capable models arrive with more provider-side retention, not less. That validates duet's premise. It also means the audit log is not a moat, because vendors will ship customer-held logs too. The durable differentiator is the stronger claim that the frontier model never needed the plaintext at all. That claim can be tested, the academic literature has not yet measured it for agentic coding, and whoever publishes the first credible, independent leakage measurement on code sets the bar others will be judged against.

Pitched as "privacy," duet is a costlier alternative to contracts banks have already signed. Pitched as frontier-grade agents on the data banks currently exclude — card-bearing code paths, customer fixtures and logs, KYC tooling, repositories under Singapore or EU sovereignty rules — its premium buys work that otherwise does not happen at all, and the right comparison is the air-gapped tools that give up the agentic tier, not Copilot. The sequence that would move a bank follows from that framing: make the local model do the reading, publish per-class detector recall and semantic leakage on code, commission an independent audit of egress completeness, bring the premium toward the range banks pay for locality, and find an integrator or commercial entity that can carry certifications, support and DORA-grade contract terms.

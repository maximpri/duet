# How large banks use GenAI and AI coding tools while protecting private data (state as of Sept 2026)

Research date: 2026-09-25. Dates are given for every fact because the landscape moved fast (2023 bans, then 2024 in-house platforms, 2025 enterprise vendor deals and coding copilots, 2026 coding agents in sandboxes). Source quality: bank press releases, regulator reports and first-tier press (CNBC, American Banker, Bloomberg, Forbes, VentureBeat, TechCrunch) are treated as reliable. Aggregator or vendor-marketing sites (Klover, reruption, fautons, nanonets, hubpy, aiproplaybook) are flagged where they are the only source. Several CNBC, Forbes and OpenAI pages returned HTTP 403, so for those the facts come from search-result excerpts of the same pages and are marked "(via search excerpt)".

## Q1. Bank by bank: internal GenAI platforms, models and vendors, deployment, data controls, coding-assistant adoption

### Takeaway
The dominant pattern at large banks is an **in-house "model broker" platform** (JPMorgan LLM Suite, GS AI Platform, Citi Stylus/Assist, UBS Red, DBS-GPT, BNP LLM-as-a-Service, Deutsche dbLumina). It sits between employees and several frontier and open-weight models, runs inside the bank's perimeter or tenancy, and connects to internal data. In parallel, banks sign **enterprise vendor deals with contractual no-training or zero-retention terms**: OpenAI (Morgan Stanley, BBVA, NatWest, Santander, CBA, Nubank), Microsoft 365 Copilot (Barclays, UBS, Lloyds, ING, Deutsche), Google (Wells Fargo, Deutsche, Lloyds, BNP, HSBC), Anthropic (JPMorgan, Goldman). The European banks most sensitive to sovereignty (BNP Paribas, HSBC) also **self-host Mistral models on their own infrastructure** for sensitive processes. Coding assistants are now near-universal: tens of thousands of developers per bank, mostly GitHub Copilot. In 2025–26 this escalated to autonomous agents (Devin at Goldman, Citi and Nubank; Claude Code at JPMorgan inside a locked-down "Devspace"). Self-reported productivity gains cluster at 10–20% for copilots.

#### Morgan Stanley
- **AI @ Morgan Stanley Assistant** launched Sept 2023. It is built on OpenAI GPT-4 over about 100,000 internal research documents for roughly 16,000 advisors — [CNBC 18 Sep 2023](https://www.cnbc.com/2023/09/18/morgan-stanley-chatgpt-financial-advisors.html).
- **Zero data retention**: according to OpenAI's case study, "OpenAI's zero data retention policy" addressed Morgan Stanley's security concerns, so that proprietary data used in prompts and retrieval is not used to train public models — [OpenAI case study (via search excerpt; page 403)](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/). The same source reports over 98% adoption among advisor teams.
- **AI @ Morgan Stanley Debrief** (June 2024) uses Whisper and GPT-4. Also AskResearchGPT — [Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/morgan-stanley-research-announces-askresearchgpt).
- **DevGen.AI** (launched Jan 2025) was built in-house on OpenAI GPT models and trained on the firm's proprietary codebase. It translates legacy code (e.g., COBOL) into plain-English specs. By June 2025 it had reviewed 9M lines of code and saved about 280,000 developer hours across roughly 15,000 developers. Mike Pizzi's quote summary: commercial coding tools "lack expertise in older or company-specific programming languages" — [Slashdot summary of WSJ](https://developers.slashdot.org/story/25/06/04/1233253/morgan-stanley-says-its-ai-tool-processed-9-million-lines-of-legacy-code-this-year-and-saved-280000-developer-hours); [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/morgan-stanley-builds-ai-tool-that-fixes-major-coding-issue/492697).

### Inferences
- There are three deployment archetypes. (1) **In-house broker over vendor APIs** (JPMorgan, Goldman, Citi, UBS Red, Deutsche dbLumina): the bank owns the UI, retrieval, logging and routing, while frontier models are called under enterprise or zero-retention terms. (2) **Vendor SaaS at scale** (ChatGPT Enterprise at BBVA, CBA, Santander; M365 Copilot at Barclays, UBS, Lloyds): contractual controls instead of architectural ones. (3) **Self-hosted open or sovereign weights** (BNP LLMaaS and Mistral, HSBC and Mistral, Capital One Llama): used where data sensitivity or sovereignty dominates. Most large banks run all three at once, which implies a routing decision per data class.
- Coding tools are the most aggressive frontier. Banks went from banning ChatGPT (2023) to mandating coding assistants for 65,000 technologists at JPMorgan (2026). Coding agents are contained through sandboxes (JPMorgan Devspace), no-deploy rules and human review (Citi), internal-only RAG (Citi), and spend caps (JPMorgan). No bank found publicly says code is sent only to on-prem models; source code is apparently treated as acceptable to send to vetted vendor clouds under enterprise terms.
- Reported productivity numbers are self-reported and inconsistent (5–15% at Citi for Copilot; 10–20% JPMorgan/Evident; 20% BofA; up to 30% Santander; "3–4x" or "2–20x" for agents). They should be treated as marketing-grade.

### Gaps
- No bank publishes its internal data-classification matrix for which class may go to which model. BNP's "certain data categories still excluded" is the most explicit public statement found.
- The Morgan Stanley zero-retention wording comes from an OpenAI marketing page (403 on fetch); the exact contractual terms are not public.
- Goldman's platform architecture ("behind firewall", "local or secure API") is described only by aggregators. The CNBC and Forbes originals were blocked (403).
- Specific coding-tool vendors are unknown for BofA, Barclays, NatWest, Capital One and CBA.
- ING's "AI co-pilot missed a bug" anecdote appeared in a search excerpt without a clear primary source, so it was excluded.

## Q2. Early bans and restrictions (2023) and how policy evolved; prohibited data classes; redaction/DLP gateways; on-prem open-weight models for sensitive data

### Takeaway
In Feb 2023 nearly every large US bank (JPMorgan, Citi, Goldman, BofA, Wells Fargo) and Deutsche Bank blocked or limited ChatGPT under existing third-party-software controls, citing data leakage and compliance rather than any incident. From 2024 to 2026 the bans were replaced by sanctioned enterprise channels: in-house platforms, enterprise contracts with no-training or zero-retention terms, PII scrubbing in front of cloud models, and self-hosted models for the most sensitive processes. Consumer or unapproved AI tools remain blocked ("shadow AI"). Publicly named prohibited data categories are rare. They are client data and sensitive production data (BNP), PII (Wells Fargo, Deutsche's Paula), and, in vendor-reported DLP deployments, client portfolios, trading strategies, MNPI and deal data.

### Cited Findings
- **Feb 2023 wave**: Citigroup, Bank of America, Deutsche Bank, Goldman Sachs and Wells Fargo joined JPMorgan in restricting ChatGPT — [Bloomberg 24 Feb 2023](https://www.bloomberg.com/news/articles/2023-02-24/citigroup-goldman-sachs-join-chatgpt-crackdown-fn-reports); [Forbes 24 Feb 2023](https://www.forbes.com/sites/brianbushard/2023/02/24/workers-chatgpt-use-restricted-at-more-banks-including-goldman-citigroup/).
  - JPMorgan's move "was not triggered by any incident and was part of controls around third-party software".
  - BofA added ChatGPT to its unauthorized-apps list alongside WhatsApp; new technology must be vetted before use in communications.
  - Deutsche Bank disabled ChatGPT as standard practice for third-party sites "aimed at protecting the bank from data leakage".
  - Citi and Goldman applied an "automatic restriction on third-party software".
  - Wells Fargo was "imposing usage limits" while considering "safe and effective ways" to use chatbots.
  - Sources: [Retail Banker International](https://www.retailbankerinternational.com/news/banking-majors-bar-chatgpt-use/); [Tech.co](https://tech.co/news/wall-street-banks-ban-ai-chatgpt).
- **Evolution, same banks by 2025–26**: JPMorgan LLM Suite for 200k+ users with coding tools mandatory for 65k technologists; Goldman GS AI Assistant firmwide; Citi Stylus/Assist and Devin; BofA Erica for Employees at 90%; Wells Fargo Agentspace to 215k; Deutsche at 80% workforce GenAI usage. See the Q1 citations.
- **Data-class restrictions stated publicly**:
  - BNP Paribas "had not placed client data or production environments containing sensitive data in public cloud" (2025). Under its new Google deal, "certain data categories still excluded" — [The Asian Banker, Sept 2026](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/bnp-paribas-adds-google-cloud-ai-while-retaining-limits-on-public-cloud-data).
  - Wells Fargo: "no sensitive data ever reaches the model", and "All the computations and detokenization, everything is on our end" — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/wells-fargos-ai-assistant-just-crossed-245-million-interactions-with-zero-humans-in-the-loop-and-zero-pii-to-the-llm).
  - Deutsche's Paula chatbot "anonymises personal information" — [db.com](https://www.db.com/what-we-do/focus-topics/tech/index?language_id=1).
  - Morgan Stanley Debrief records client meetings only "with client consent" — [Morgan Stanley](https://www.morganstanley.com/press-releases/ai-at-morgan-stanley-debrief-launch).
- **DLP to public AI (vendor-reported, unnamed bank)**: a "regional investment bank" used an enterprise DLP to block hundreds of attempted transfers to public AI platforms. Its classes were public, internal reference, client-confidential and trading-sensitive. Blocked content included "client portfolio information, trading strategies and execution models, material non-public information, and deal-related data protected by confidentiality agreements" — [CFOtech](https://cfotech.news/story/bank-blocks-confidential-data-leaks-to-public-ai-tools); [openPR](https://www.openpr.com/news/4634187/investment-bank-blocks-hundreds-of-confidential-data-transfers). (Vendor PR; the bank is unnamed; treat as illustrative.)
- **On-prem or self-hosted models for sensitive data**:
  - BNP LLMaaS in group data centers, and Mistral on-prem for KYC — [FF News](https://ffnews.com/news/bnp-paribas-provides-its-businesses-with-an-llm-as-a-service-platform-to-accelerate-the-industrialization-of-generative-ai-use-cases); [BNP](https://group.bnpparibas/en/press-release/bnp-paribas-and-mistral-ai-extend-their-partnership-to-support-the-next-phase-of-generative-ai-deployment-within-the-group).
  - HSBC self-hosted Mistral "on HSBC's internal technology systems" — [HSBC](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2025/hsbc-and-mistral-ai-join-forces-to-accelerate-ai-adoption-across-global-bank).
  - Capital One fine-tuned open-weight Llama — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/orchestration/why-capital-one-built-its-multi-agent-ai-platform-around-open-weight-models).
  - Wells Fargo uses local speech-to-text and a local SLM for PII detection — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/wells-fargos-ai-assistant-just-crossed-245-million-interactions-with-zero-humans-in-the-loop-and-zero-pii-to-the-llm).
- **Coding-agent containment**:
  - Citi agents cannot deploy code and are restricted to internal repos via RAG — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/citi-is-rolling-out-agentic-ai-to-its-40-000-developers).
  - JPMorgan moves Claude Code into a "more secure" Devspace environment — [Newsquawk/BI](https://www.newsquawk.com/headlines/jpmorgan-jpm-introduced-usd-2k-monthly-claude-code-spending-limits-for-some-engineers-and-is-rolling-out-a-more-secure-devspace-environment-business-insider-reports).

### Inferences
- The operating rule the evidence implies: **public or consumer AI is blocked; the enterprise tenant or in-house broker is allowed for internal and confidential data; the most sensitive regulated data (client PII in customer flows, KYC, production data) either never reaches an external model (tokenized or scrubbed first) or goes only to self-hosted models.** The European banks (BNP, HSBC) make this explicit. US banks mostly rely on enterprise contracts plus in-house brokers.
- The ban-to-platform path was driven by third-party risk management (vendor vetting, contract terms), not by new AI-specific rules.

### Gaps
- No primary-source bank policy document was found listing prohibited data classes by model tier. Such policies are internal.
- No public evidence was found on whether JPMorgan, Goldman or Citi use a commercial redaction/DLP gateway product in front of LLM Suite, the GS AI Platform or Stylus. The brokers presumably log and filter, but this is not documented.

## Q3. Statements from bank CISOs and CTOs on AI data risk and third-party risk

### Takeaway
The most-cited statement is JPMorgan CISO Patrick Opet's open letter to suppliers (26 Apr 2025). It frames SaaS and AI integrations, especially OAuth-token-style "direct integration" into internal data, as a systemic risk and demands secure-by-default, transparency, and rejection of unsafe integration models. Bank CTOs emphasize human review, internal-only grounding and platform-level guardrails for coding agents.

### Cited Findings
- **Opet letter (26 Apr 2025)** — [JPMorganChase](https://www.jpmorganchase.com/about/technology/blog/open-letter-to-our-suppliers):
  - "The modern 'software as a service' (SaaS) delivery model is quietly enabling cyber attackers and — as its adoption grows — is creating a substantial vulnerability that is weakening the global economic system."
  - On AI: "explosive growth of new value-bearing services in data management, automation, artificial intelligence, and AI agents amplifies and rapidly distributes these risks".
  - His example is an "AI-driven calendar optimization service" where "direct integration grants attackers unprecedented access to confidential data".
  - On authentication: "inadequately secured authentication tokens vulnerable to theft and reuse" and "opaque fourth-party vendor dependencies silently expanding this same risk".
  - On authorization: modern integrations create "single-factor explicit trust between systems on the internet and private internal resources".
  - Demands: "Providers must urgently reprioritize security, placing it equal to or above launching new products"; "secure by default configurations"; transparency of risks and controls; and "reject these integration models without better solutions".
- Commentary: [Security Magazine](https://www.securitymagazine.com/articles/101591-jpmorgan-chase-and-co-ciso-writes-open-letter-to-third-party-suppliers); [Infosecurity Magazine](https://www.infosecurity-magazine.com/news/jpmorgan-ciso-warns-saas-security/); [SC Media](https://www.scworld.com/perspective/why-patrick-opets-letter-hit-a-nerve). One vendor-oriented summary claims JPMorgan now requires "AI assurance documentation" (training data, model development, fairness, monitoring) from suppliers — [CPOstrategy](https://cpostrategy.media/blog/2025/05/02/jp-morgan-open-letter-highlights-security-vulnerabilities-of-ai-procurement/). This is **not in the letter text itself**; treat it as interpretation. ReversingLabs reports suppliers' remediation times dropped sharply after the letter — [RL Blog](https://www.reversinglabs.com/blog/opet-jpmc-software-trust-debt-rsac) (vendor blog).
- **Citi CTO David Griffiths (Jul 2025)** on coding agents: "They need technology controls… quality checks, automated testing"; "we're certainly not allowing any kind of deployment of code by the agents"; agents "cannot swim out into the web" — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/citi-is-rolling-out-agentic-ai-to-its-40-000-developers).
- **Wells Fargo CIO Chintan Mehta (Apr 2025)**: "The orchestration layer talks to the model. We're the filters in front and behind" — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/wells-fargos-ai-assistant-just-crossed-245-million-interactions-with-zero-humans-in-the-loop-and-zero-pii-to-the-llm).
- **Morgan Stanley's Mike Pizzi**: legacy code "weakens security". He justified building on proprietary code because commercial tools lack firm-specific language expertise — [Entrepreneur](https://www.entrepreneur.com/business-news/morgan-stanley-builds-ai-tool-that-fixes-major-coding-issue/492697).
- **JPMorgan CAO Derek Waldron**: "There is a value gap between what the technology is capable of and the ability to fully capture that in an enterprise" — [CNBC Sept 2025 (via search excerpt)](https://www.cnbc.com/2025/09/30/jpmorgan-chase-fully-ai-connected-megabank.html).
- **Dimon letter (Apr 2026)** lists AI among top risks alongside geopolitics and private markets — [CNBC 6 Apr 2026](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/04/06/jpmorgan-ceo-jamie-dimon-annual-letter-risks.html).

### Inferences
- Opet's concern maps directly onto agentic AI: agents that hold OAuth tokens into mail, calendar, code and data stores are exactly the "direct integration" pattern he criticizes. This helps explain why JPMorgan wraps Claude Code in Devspace instead of granting tools direct access.

### Gaps
- No public CISO statements were found from Goldman, Citi, BofA or the European banks specifically on LLM data leakage in 2025–26.

## Q4. Surveys of financial institutions on GenAI adoption barriers (privacy, model risk)

### Takeaway
Regulator and consultancy surveys consistently put data protection and privacy at or near the top of barriers, with third-party and concentration risk rising. The Bank of England and FCA found data protection and privacy to be the largest perceived regulatory constraint, and four of the top five perceived risks are data-related.

### Cited Findings
- **Bank of England and FCA, 3rd AI survey (21 Nov 2024; 118 firms)** — [Bank of England](https://www.bankofengland.co.uk/report/2024/artificial-intelligence-in-uk-financial-services-2024); [FCA research note](https://www.fca.org.uk/publications/research-notes/ai-uk-financial-services):
  - "75% of firms are already using artificial intelligence (AI), with a further 10% planning to use AI over the next three years."
  - "Foundation models form 17% of all AI use cases."
  - "A third of all AI use cases are third-party implementations" (up from 17% in 2022).
  - "The top three third-party providers account for 73%, 44%, and 33% of all reported cloud, model, and data providers respectively."
  - "46% of respondent firms reported having only 'partial understanding' of the AI technologies they use versus 34%… 'complete understanding'."
  - "The largest perceived regulatory constraint to the use of AI is data protection and privacy". 23% rate it a large constraint, and 33% cite high regulatory burden for data protection — [Stephenson Harwood](https://www.stephensonharwood.com/insights/the-bank-of-england-and-the-fcas-survey-on-ai-in-uk-financial-services/).
  - "Of the top five perceived current risks, four are related to data" (privacy/protection, quality, security, bias).
  - Largest non-regulatory constraint: "safety, security and robustness of AI models".
  - Biggest expected increase in risk over three years: third-party dependencies, model complexity, and embedded or "hidden" models.
- **EY-Parthenon GenAI in Banking survey (2025)**: 77% of banks have launched or soft-launched GenAI apps (vs 61% in 2023). Barriers: 26% regulatory/compliance, **22% data privacy concerns**, 21% limited access to quality data — [EY](https://www.ey.com/en_us/insights/banking-capital-markets/ai-in-banking-ey-parthenon-genai-survey-insights).
- **Deloitte**: 72% of about 2,600 US bank customers are concerned about sharing financial information with GenAI tools — [Deloitte Insights](https://www.deloitte.com/us/en/insights/industry/financial-services/bank-customers-generative-ai-trust.html). A search excerpt also attributed "67% data security as a leading concern" to a Deloitte survey. The underlying survey could not be identified, so this figure is excluded as unverified.
- **Evident AI Index 2025**: JPMorgan, Capital One and RBC rank top three; Morgan Stanley entered the top five — [Evident](https://evidentinsights.com/bankingbrief/heres-the-2025-evident-ai-index/).
  - Banks "are equipping thousands of developers with AI agents… and are already reporting 10-20% productivity gains, on average".
  - Engineer and developer augmentation use cases grew 8x since Feb 2024 and are "fast becoming table stakes" — [Evident use-case trends](https://evidentinsights.com/insights/use-case-trends-q4-2025).
  - 32 tracked banks report use cases with tangible outcomes — [Evident Outcomes Report](https://evidentinsights.com/insights/outcomes-report/).

### Inferences
- The regulator data shows two tensions: third-party implementations and vendor concentration are rising, while understanding of the models is only partial. That is the systemic version of Opet's single-firm complaint, and it motivates banks' push for in-house brokers and self-hosted options.

### Gaps
- McKinsey and American Banker bank-specific GenAI barrier surveys for 2025–26 were not retrieved.
- No 2025–26 follow-up to the BoE/FCA survey was found.

## Q5. Named hybrid approaches and privacy middleware (sensitive data on-prem or scrubbed, reasoning in the cloud)

### Takeaway
The clearest named hybrid is **Wells Fargo's Fargo**. Speech is transcribed locally, PII is detected and tokenized by an internal small language model, only de-identified text goes to Google Gemini Flash for intent extraction, and detokenization and API calls stay in-house. BNP Paribas, HSBC and Deutsche Bank use split architectures by data class, and JPMorgan, Goldman and Citi use in-house brokers as the policy enforcement point.

### Cited Findings
- **Wells Fargo Fargo (Apr 2025)**: "Speech is transcribed locally with a speech-to-text model". Text is "scrubbed and tokenized… including a small language model (SLM) for personally identifiable information (PII) detection". Only then is Gemini Flash 2.0 called; "No sensitive data ever reaches the model"; "All the computations and detokenization, everything is on our end. Our APIs… none of them pass through the LLM". Volume went from 21.3M interactions in 2023 to 245M in 2024 — [VentureBeat](https://venturebeat.com/ai/wells-fargos-ai-assistant-just-crossed-245-million-interactions-with-zero-humans-in-the-loop-and-zero-pii-to-the-llm); [AIM](https://aimmediahouse.com/market-industry/fargo-handled-more-than-200-million-requests-without-sending-customer-data-to-an-llm).
- **BNP Paribas**: on-prem LLMaaS and Mistral on-prem for KYC, with Google Cloud and Gemini for LLM@CIB under rules excluding certain data categories — [The Asian Banker](https://www.theasianbanker.com/updates-and-articles/bnp-paribas-adds-google-cloud-ai-while-retaining-limits-on-public-cloud-data).
- **HSBC**: self-hosted Mistral on internal systems alongside an expanding Google Cloud partnership — [HSBC](https://www.hsbc.com/news-and-views/news/media-releases/2025/hsbc-and-mistral-ai-join-forces-to-accelerate-ai-adoption-across-global-bank); [AI News](https://www.artificialintelligence-news.com/news/hsbc-google-cloud-ai-partnership/).
- **Deutsche Bank**: Google Cloud and Gemini for dbLumina; the Paula chatbot anonymizes personal information — [db.com](https://www.db.com/what-we-do/focus-topics/tech/index?language_id=1).
- **Morgan Stanley**: a contractual approach rather than an architectural one. It uses OpenAI with zero data retention plus internal RAG — [OpenAI (via search excerpt)](https://openai.com/index/morgan-stanley/).
- **Academic framing**: "Separation of Concerns for Privacy-Preserving LLM Adoption: A Banking Architecture Framework" (IEEE/ACM workshop on SE challenges in financial firms, 2026) — [ACM DL](https://dl.acm.org/doi/10.1145/3786170.3788387) (not fetched).

### Inferences
- The Wells Fargo pattern (local SLM for PII detection plus tokenization, then cloud LLM on de-identified text, then local rehydration) is the one publicly documented, production-scale, named example of "local privacy layer, cloud reasoning" in banking.

### Gaps
- No bank publicly documents a hybrid pattern for **coding** (e.g., local model reads proprietary code, cloud model reasons). Coding tools appear to send code to vetted vendor clouds under enterprise terms.

## Q6. Incidents and bans

### Takeaway
The main post-2023 incident found is a **shadow-AI breach at Community Bank (CB Financial Services), May 2026**. An employee fed customer names, dates of birth and SSNs into an unauthorized AI app, which triggered an SEC 8-K, regulator notice and GLBA customer notice. Beyond the 2023 bans, no public data-leak incident involving a sanctioned platform at a large bank was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Community Bank (PA, OH, WV; parent CB Financial Services)**: an employee used "an unauthorized artificial intelligence-based software application", exposing customer names, dates of birth and Social Security numbers. The AI app was not named and the number of affected customers was not disclosed — [TechCrunch 12 May 2026](https://techcrunch.com/2026/05/12/us-bank-discloses-security-lapse-after-sharing-customer-data-with-ai-app/).
  - American Banker notes a single employee act triggered SEC disclosure, a 36-hour notice to the prudential regulator, and GLBA customer notice — [American Banker](https://www.americanbanker.com/news/a-bank-breaks-its-silence-on-its-shadow-ai-breach).
